## What the edit pass taught us

- A stronger lead often comes from reading further into the evidence, not from adding exciting language.
- Select one explanatory thread. Regulatory inventories, prize amounts and equipment lists usually dilute the story.
- Place agency and time after the impact when the listener needs them. Observation dates and publication dates do different jobs.
- Ending variety matters: a deadline, a conditional outlook, a mechanism or the limits of a finding can each resolve a story.
- Removing dead air does not require faster speech. The script should carry its own rhythm; pictures can hold under it.
- An accepted line can remain. Revision is not a requirement to replace good copy with something less precise.
- A source check can sharpen a deadline or reveal a better angle. It does not automatically make old evidence current.
