Implemented September 28, 2026.

The viewing canvas leads. Charcoal surfaces, a restrained sage accent, lighter typography, subtle borders, and fewer simultaneous labels replace the denser presentation. A floating transport stays inside the frame. The headline and source sit beneath it, and upcoming stories occupy a single horizontal strip. Fullscreen keeps title, captions, and playback controls separated.

Nine new illustrations use a single subject and generous open space. Swift has its own satellite; Crew-13 a separate capsule; isotope research a river ecosystem; Yellowstone a monitoring station; Lake Chelan a conceptual depth cutaway; Maryland's bird record a warbler; the Havel prize an imagined award and letter; stablecoin proposals an abstract token/reserve composition; cybersecurity training a learning workspace. The original star nursery and ceramic illustration remain exclusive to their original stories.

Each illustration is bound to the story ID and exact narration script. Public disclosures distinguish conceptual hardware, invented trophies, illustrative wildlife, and lake graphics from evidence. File hashes detect changed images or video, and publication rejects image reuse across different stories. Old assets, prompts, and editions are retained. New visuals have 12-second CPU-composed loops with a gentle 2% zoom. No event footage is claimed.

Topic concentration is reduced without dropping the viewer's preferences. When at least three selected topics have ready stories, a lap interleaves the topics and includes up to two stories from each. Longer topic queues advance on later laps. With one or two available topics, all matching stories remain in the lap. Coming up reflects the actual planned next stories, including the next lap when needed.
