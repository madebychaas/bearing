# Bearing — Product Vision

## The idea

**Bearing helps people get their bearings.**

The world does not suffer from a shortage of headlines, facts, updates, clips, feeds, posts, alerts, and commentary.

Bearing exists to help people make sense of them.

It turns reporting into an intelligible viewing experience: what happened, why it matters, how the pieces fit together, and what deserves attention next.

**News as orientation, not performance.**

## The experience

**Open Bearing. Pick your lane. Understand what's going on. Keep going if you want.**

A Bearing session should feel easy to enter and equally easy to leave.

The viewer chooses what they want to understand. Bearing does the work of gathering, shaping, sequencing, explaining, and presenting the relevant reporting.

The viewer should not need to operate a newsroom, browse dozens of stories, or construct the larger picture themselves.

Once they feel oriented, they can:

- keep watching,
- explore another lane,
- go deeper on a story,
- or leave.

Leaving informed is a successful session.

Bearing does not need to maximize time spent. It should maximize understanding.

## Pick your lane

Bearing should give viewers meaningful control over the part of the world they want help understanding.

A lane may represent an interest, subject, place, concern, or other coherent area of attention.

The exact lane system can evolve. The important thing is the interaction:

**The viewer chooses the territory. Bearing provides the orientation.**

Personalization should be explicit and understandable. Avoid hidden engagement optimization that gradually changes the product around a user without their awareness.

## Produced, not generated

**Produced, not generated.**

AI is part of Bearing's production machinery. It should not be the aesthetic.

A finished Bearing story should feel intentionally made: written, edited, voiced, visualized, scored, paced, and assembled as one coherent piece of programming.

It should not feel like:

- narration placed over generated images,
- a slideshow assembled from model outputs,
- disconnected AI assets,
- or a technology demonstration.

Every production choice should serve the story.

Scripts lead with the new development and its human impact. For a report-driven story, the opening establishes that the takeaway comes from a new report; the next sentence or two names the source and verified release weekday when useful. Each segment must make sense on its own, from opening through explanation to closing.

News needs rhythm and flow. Keep clear articulation and natural sentence breathing, without long empty pauses or an opening fade that swallows words. Give graphics reading time through relevant picture holds, not dead air. Reveals follow normal reading order, top to bottom and left to right. Music supports the editorial subject and voice, without sentimental or dramatic decoration.

Treat the picture as video, not a document or dashboard. Avoid permanent headers, eyebrows, source strips and footers over original graphics. Use the frame confidently: scale an explanation up, split it into distinct visual beats or rethink the composition when it feels cramped. An opening made of words needs an authored reveal and visual emphasis, not a preloaded left-aligned title. Use cuts and purposeful movement; cross dissolves should be occasional editorial choices rather than the default between scenes. Keep third-party courtesy credits when required, and make factual sources available behind the story.

Give each visual time to communicate. A representative image can establish the opening while one title gradually comes together with the first sentence; leave that title behind when the body begins. Alternate imagery and explanatory graphics according to what the narration needs, rather than turning every phrase into another text card. When explaining a process, let its parts accumulate in a clear reading order and remain visible long enough to understand their relationship. Keep qualifications and required steps connected to that process. For a closing explanation, use visual cause and effect to make the consequences understandable, without implying certainty the reporting does not support.

The October 1 student-loan revision (`9c4fe29dbfb3a18916ea`) is the viewer-accepted reference for this production approach. Keep it tight, dynamic and moving with editorial judgment: move when a new idea, relationship or emphasis needs attention; hold when the viewer needs to read, connect or absorb what is already there. Cut once that picture has done its job. Motion should direct attention, and a hold should still carry the story forward under narration. Do not rush the voice to create energy or animate continuously to imply polish. Preserve the approach, not a fixed shot order: image openings, title builds and three-column diagrams are choices to earn story by story.

## Product principles

### Understanding over volume

More stories do not automatically create a better news experience.

Bearing should prioritize the information necessary to understand what is happening and how it fits together.

### Context over recap

Repeating the latest facts is not enough.

Good Bearing storytelling helps the viewer understand significance, relationships, consequences, uncertainty, and what to watch next.

### Orientation is editorial value

A story has succeeded when the viewer is better situated afterward than before.

Bearing should continually ask:

- What does the viewer need to understand?
- What is the important context?
- What connects this development to the larger story?
- What remains uncertain?
- What should they pay attention to next?

### Calm without being soft

Bearing should feel measured, clear, and humane.

Calm does not mean minimizing seriousness. Urgency should come from the facts, not from visual noise, breathless writing, alarmist music, or constant breaking-news treatment.

### Watch first

The central experience is watching coherent programming.

Production systems, sourcing mechanics, preferences, provenance, controls, and additional reporting should be available when useful without overtaking the story itself.

### Source-grounded

AI may transform, organize, explain, illustrate, narrate, and assemble supported reporting.

AI is not itself a source of facts.

Factual claims should remain tied to identifiable reporting or primary-source evidence. Synthetic visuals should not masquerade as documentary evidence.

### Personal without manipulation

Bearing should respond to explicit interests and choices.

It should not quietly optimize itself around addictive engagement behavior.

The product should help viewers understand the world, not find reasons to keep them from leaving.

### One coherent experience

Features, formats, and surfaces should reinforce the same simple promise.

When adding something makes Bearing feel more like a dashboard, production console, feed reader, or collection of disconnected formats, reconsider whether it belongs in the primary experience.

## The standard

A Bearing story should have:

- a reason for existing,
- a clear editorial point,
- a beginning,
- progression,
- useful context,
- visual logic,
- intentional pacing,
- and a meaningful ending or handoff.

The viewer should come away thinking:

**Now I understand what is going on.**

Not:

**That was impressive AI.**
